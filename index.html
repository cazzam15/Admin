Section A — Customer Service & Corporate Image
Q1 · Multiple Choice
Which of the following is a BENEFIT of good customer service for an organisation?
AIncreased customer complaints
BCustomer loyalty and increased profits/sales
CHigher staff turnover and absenteeism
DNegative publicity for the organisation
B — Customer loyalty and increased profits. Benefits of good customer service: customer loyalty, reduced complaints, increased profits/sales, good publicity, lower staff turnover, competitive edge (increased market share), improved efficiency.
Q2 · Short Answer (3 marks)
Describe THREE features of a corporate image that an organisation might use.
Model answer (1 mark each, any 3):
• Standardised colours, fonts, and graphics across all documents. (1)
• A company logo used on all materials. (1)
• A slogan or tagline. (1)
• Staff uniform. (1)
• Consistent house style in all IT documents. (1)
• Standardised customer service responses. (1)
Section B — Legislation & Security
Q3 · Fill in the Blanks — Legislation
The Act 1974 requires employers to provide a safe working environment and carry out risk assessments.

The Act requires organisations to store personal data securely and only use it for the stated purpose.

The Act makes it illegal to access computer systems without authorisation.
Health and Safety at Work Act 1974 · Data Protection Act (GDPR) · Computer Misuse Act
Q4 · Multiple Choice
Which of the following is a BENEFIT of good file management?
AFiles take longer to find
BSaves time finding files and improves efficiency
CFiles are more likely to be duplicated
DWastes network storage space
B — Saves time and improves efficiency. Good file management benefits: saves time finding files, saves network space, reduces stress, improves efficiency leading to good reputation, no duplication so files are more up to date.
Q5 · Short Answer (2 marks)
State TWO consequences of using UNRELIABLE internet sources of information in business.
Model answer (1 mark each, any 2):
• Wrong decisions are made by the organisation. (1)
• Meetings are missed (e.g. wrong venue address used). (1)
• The organisation loses money. (1)
• The organisation gets a bad reputation. (1)
• The organisation loses opportunities. (1)

Section A — Spreadsheets
Q1 · Multiple Choice
A formula must always refer to the same cell (e.g. a tax rate) regardless of where it is copied. Which type of cell reference should be used?
ARelative reference (e.g. B2)
BAbsolute reference (e.g. $B$2)
CNamed range
DA relative reference with no $ signs
B — Absolute reference ($B$2). The dollar signs lock both the column and row, preventing them from changing when the formula is copied. Used whenever a formula must always reference the same cell (e.g. a VAT rate, discount percentage).
Q2 · Fill in the Blanks — Spreadsheet Functions
=(B2:B20) — finds the highest value in the range
=(B2:B20) — finds the average of all values
=(D5>=50,"Pass","Fail") — tests a condition and returns a value
=(B2:B20) — counts cells containing numbers
=MAX (highest) · =AVERAGE (mean) · =IF (conditional) · =COUNT (count numbers). Also know: =MIN (lowest), =SUM (total), =COUNTA (count any data).
Section B — Databases
Q3 · Multiple Choice
A database search needs to find all employees with a salary GREATER THAN OR EQUAL TO £25,000. Which operator should be used?
ASalary > 25000
BSalary = 25000
CSalary >= 25000
DSalary NOT 25000
C — Salary >= 25000. >= means greater than OR equal to — finds all records where salary is £25,000 or more. > alone would exclude those earning exactly £25,000. = only finds exactly £25,000.
Q4 · Short Answer (3 marks)
Describe the difference between a database FORM, REPORT, and LABEL.
Model answer (1 mark each):
Form: displays and allows input of ONE record at a time in an easy-to-use layout — used for data entry and editing. (1)
Report: presents selected data from one or more tables in a professional, formatted layout with headers/footers — used for printing and presenting data. (1)
Label: produces address labels or name badges from database records — one label per record in a grid format ready for label sheets. (1)

Section A — Word Processing & Documents
Q1 · Multiple Choice
A manager needs to send personalised letters to 500 customers using their names and addresses from a database. What is the MOST efficient method?
AMail merge — combining the letter template with the database
BTyping each letter individually
CPhotocopying a generic letter 500 times
DEmailing a single identical letter to everyone
A — Mail merge. Mail merge combines a main document (with merge fields) and a data source (database/spreadsheet). It automatically produces 500 personalised letters — each addressed individually — far more efficiently than typing each one separately.
Q2 · Short Answer (2 marks)
State the difference between an AGENDA and MINUTES of a meeting.
Agenda: circulated BEFORE the meeting — lists the topics to be discussed, time, date, and venue. (1)
Minutes: written record produced AFTER the meeting — records what was discussed, decisions made, and actions agreed. (1)
Section B — Electronic Communication
Q3 · Fill in the Blanks — Email
The field sends a copy to additional recipients who are visible to all other recipients.
The field sends a hidden copy — the recipient is not visible to other recipients.
Automatically adding your name and contact details to every email is done using an email
CC (carbon copy) — visible to all. BCC (blind carbon copy) — hidden from all other recipients. Email signature — auto-appended contact details at the bottom of every email.
Q4 · Short Answer (3 marks)
Describe THREE benefits of using an e-diary compared to a paper diary.
Model answer (1 mark each, any 3):
• Automatic reminders — alerts sent so appointments are not missed. (1)
• Recurring appointments set once and repeat automatically. (1)
• Easy to share with colleagues — view their availability. (1)
• Accessible on multiple devices (phone, tablet, PC). (1)
• Can be backed up — no risk of losing it. (1)
• Integrated with email — meeting invites sent automatically. (1)

🏢 Administration Theory

Admin Tasks (Duties)
Creating/updating documents · Booking rooms/venues · Organising travel & accommodation · Filing/storing documents · Taking minutes · Dealing with correspondence · Supporting events

Organised · Good communicator · Team player · Cooperative · Ability to multitask · Good IT skills · Patient · Tactful/discreet · Approachable · Willing to learn

Customer loyalty · Reduced complaints · Increased profits/sales · Good publicity · Lower staff turnover · Competitive edge · Improved efficiency/productivity
Poor Customer Service — Consequences
Increased complaints · Decreased profits · Negative publicity · Higher staff turnover · Decreased market share · Lower efficiency · Increased waste
Good File Management — Benefits
Saves time finding files · Saves network space · Less employee stress · Improved efficiency → good reputation · No duplication → files up to date
Poor File Management — Consequences
Wastes time · Wastes network space · More stress · Lower efficiency → poor reputation · Duplication → files less up to date
Corporate Image — Features & Benefits
Features: standardised colours/fonts/graphics · logo · slogan · staff uniform · house style across all documents
Benefits: instantly recognisable brand · professional reputation · consistent staff = customers treated fairly
Reliable Internet Sources
Features: non-biased · accurate · regularly updated · reputable author/organisation · complete/sufficient
Benefits: good decisions · good reputation · gains opportunities
Unreliable → wrong decisions · bad reputation · lost money/opportunities

Information: usernames/passwords, encryption, access levels, data backups
Electronic Communication Methods
Email · E-diary · Blog · Podcast/vodcast · Presentation · Social media · Video/audio conferencing · Webinar · Website
Each has different features, uses, and benefits for workplace communication

📊 Spreadsheets

Key Functions
=SUM(range) — total  |  =AVERAGE(range) — mean
=MAX(range) — highest value  |  =MIN(range) — lowest value
=COUNT(range) — count cells with numbers  |  =COUNTA — count any data
=IF(condition,"true","false") — conditional test
IF Function Syntax
=IF(logical_test, value_if_true, value_if_false)
Example: =IF(D5>=50,"Pass","Fail")
If D5 is 50 or more, show "Pass", otherwise show "Fail"
Cell References
Relative: B2 — changes when copied to a new position
Absolute: $B$2 — stays fixed wherever copied ($ locks column & row)
Mixed: $B2 (column locked) or B$2 (row locked)
Named cell: give a cell a descriptive name for use in formulas
Chart Types
Pie chart — proportions/percentages of a whole
Bar/column chart — comparing categories side by side
Line graph — showing trends over time
Always include: chart title · axis labels · data labels · legend
Print Settings
Value view — shows calculated results (what you normally see)
Formula view — shows actual formulas typed in cells (Ctrl+`)
Print with/without gridlines & row/column headings
Portrait or landscape · Fit to one page · Headers/footers
Spreadsheet Benefits
Automatic recalculation when data changes · Easy to create charts
Sort and filter data · Perform complex calculations quickly
Data can be shared and merged into other documents (mail merge)
Formulae reduce the chance of calculation errors

🗄️ Databases

Search Operators
= (equals)  |  > (greater than)  |  < (less than)
>= (greater than or equal to)  |  <= (less than or equal to)
NOT / <> (not equal to)  |  OR (either condition true)
Example: Salary >= 25000 finds everyone earning £25,000 or more
Database Objects
Table: stores raw data in rows (records) and columns (fields)
Form: input/view ONE record at a time in a user-friendly layout
Report: professional formatted printout of selected data
Label: address labels/name badges — one label per record
Query/Search: find records matching specified criteria
Relational Database
Two or more tables linked by a common field
Primary key: unique identifier — no two records have the same value
Foreign key: field that matches the primary key in another table
Benefits: reduces data duplication · easier to update · more consistent
Sorting
Sort on ONE field: e.g. sort surnames A–Z (ascending) or Z–A (descending)
Sort on TWO fields: primary sort field, then secondary sort where values match
Example: sort by Surname (primary), then Firstname (secondary)
Database Benefits
Instant searching and sorting · Easy to update and keep accurate
Produce forms, reports, and labels · Mail merge into other documents
Control who can access which data · Reduces physical storage space
Multiple users can access simultaneously

📄 Word Processing & DTP

Business Documents
Letter · Agenda (before meeting) · Minutes (after meeting)
Itinerary · Business report · Form · Poster · Leaflet
Booklet · Newsletter · Name badge
Mail Merge Process
1. Create main document with merge fields (<<FirstName>>)
2. Connect to data source (database or spreadsheet)
3. Match merge fields to data source fields
4. Preview and complete merge — one copy per record
Used for: letters, labels, name badges, certificates
Business Report Structure
Title · Author · Date
→ Introduction/Background (purpose and scope)
→ Findings (main research and data)
→ Conclusions (what the findings mean)
→ Recommendations (suggested actions)
→ Appendix (supporting documents)
House Style
Consistent use of: fonts · font sizes · colours · margins
Logo placement · header/footer layout · document templates
Ensures all documents look professional and instantly recognisable
Contributes to and reinforces corporate image
Key Word Processing Features
Find & Replace: locate and replace text throughout a document
Comments: non-printing notes attached to specific text
Headers/footers: appear on every page — title, page numbers, date
Track changes: shows edits made by different users

📧 IT Communication

Email Fields
TO: main recipient(s) who need to act on the email
CC (Carbon Copy): additional recipients — all can see who is CC'd
BCC (Blind Carbon Copy): hidden from all other recipients
Subject: brief description of email content
Attachment: file(s) sent with the email
Signature: auto-added contact details at the bottom
E-Diary Features & Benefits
Schedule appointments · Set automatic reminders
Recurring appointments (set once, repeat automatically)
Print: daily/weekly/monthly calendar views
Access other users' calendars · Schedule tasks/to-do list
Benefits over paper: reminders · shareable · accessible on multiple devices · backed up
Electronic Communication Methods
Video conferencing: meetings without travel, can record, screen share
Webinar: online seminar, live Q&A, attendees join remotely
Presentation: visual delivery to groups at meetings/events
Social media: reach large audience, interactive, instant
Blog: regular updates, builds audience over time
Podcast/vodcast: audio/video content, downloadable
Internet Research Tips
Reliable sources: government (.gov.uk), NHS, universities, reputable organisations
Check: author · date updated · accuracy · bias
Use Bookmarks/Favourites to save useful sites
Always verify information before using it in business decisions
